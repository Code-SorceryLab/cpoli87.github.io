## Evolved Neural Agents as Tireless Playtesters — Al Shifan

Every level edit needs new playtests, and the code tests still pass when an edit makes a level harder or even impossible. Al's idea is to train small evolved agents once, freeze them, and keep them as tests for the level. After an edit, the frozen agents play the level again and flag what changed.

Across 130 small edits to 28 levels in 4 games, the agents caught 70 of the 76 edits that changed play, with no false alarms on 54 unchanged controls.

Al is co-supervised with Loutfouz Zaman.

## Trait Activation Steering: Keeping Small-Model NPCs in Character — Kevin Chua

<div class="row mt-3">
  <div class="col-sm mt-3 mt-md-0">
    {% include figure.liquid path="assets/posts/cascon-2026/kevin-poster.jpg" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" alt="Kevin standing next to his poster, Trait Activation Steering" %}
  </div>
  <div class="col-sm mt-3 mt-md-0">
    {% include figure.liquid path="assets/posts/cascon-2026/kevin-visitor.jpg" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" alt="Kevin pointing at a chart on his poster while a visitor listens" %}
  </div>
</div>

Small language models drift out of character in long conversations. Kevin's method, TAST, steers the model's hidden state while it runs, so a small NPC stays in character through a 20-turn attack, without any fine-tuning.

Kevin is co-supervised with Ali Neshati.
