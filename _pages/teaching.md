## CSCI 5160G Topics in Software Design (Winter 2025)

This was my first time designing and teaching a graduate course, so every element—from syllabus to assessments—had to be built from the ground up. The central goal was ambitious: guide students through the full research‐paper cycle, from defining a problem in software engineering for game development to drafting a submission‐ready manuscript. Over weekly three-hour sessions, I combined domain lectures with milestone-based checkpoints, giving iterative feedback that kept the five students on pace. Close coordination with each student’s supervisor ensured each paper sat at the intersection of the course theme and the student’s primary research focus. All final papers show clear workshop-level potential, and two projects (Annie’s and Loutfouz’s students) are already slated for extension into larger venues.
The experience has been energizing, and student engagement—especially around AI and machine-learning topics—was high. For the next offering, I plan to refine the grading scheme, streamline milestone timing to match the single weekly meeting, and add deeper AI/ML content to build on this enthusiasm.

## CSCI 4160U Interactive Media (Winter 2025)

This was my first time teaching the course, so I built the entire curriculum from the ground up. I emphasized core concepts over specific tools, switching from Unity to a lightweight open-source engine that students embraced enthusiastically. Because the cohort was already experienced, I structured the class as course-based learning: broad coverage of game-development and game-design fundamentals, with each topic paired to an in-class exercise or lab.
The assessment scaffold mirrored industry practice. Mid-semester, teams produced a “vertical slice” demo to earn detailed feedback; the final exam was a complete game created from scratch. Additional deliverables required systematic analyses of game-design decisions. The resulting projects spanned diverse themes and techniques, reflecting both the course’s wide lens and the students’ individual interests.

## CSCI 4230U Advanced Web Development (Fall 2024)

This was my first time teaching the course, so I built the entire curriculum from scratch. Instead of centering the instruction on a single framework, I concentrated on fundamental back-end concepts—then reinforced each topic with a hands-on exercise and a short quiz.
For the capstone project, students designed and implemented a complete web application that demonstrated every concept covered in class. They were free to choose any tech stack and domain, encouraging creativity, a sense of ownership, and portfolio-ready work—especially valuable for these fourth-year students preparing to enter the job market.
Overall, the outcomes were strong, but the rapidly evolving content will benefit from continuous updates and refinement before the next offering. One persistent challenge was the early time slot (8:10 a.m. – 11:00 a.m.), which occasionally affected energy levels and pacing.
