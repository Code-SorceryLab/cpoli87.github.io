What about Indies?

Created on December 01, 2021 by Cristiano Politowski

2021   ·   opinion   review

In the book “Press Reset: Ruin and Recovery in the Video Game Industry” by Jason Schreier there are some interesting quotes about indies.

“Launching an indie game is like—I just picture a lonely dude in a party hat and a folding chair,” said Dowling. “A single confetti falls from the ceiling.” Because you launch and it turns out that when you’ve spent $20,000 on marketing, nobody’s heard of you. So it takes time for people to hear of you.”

The game market is saturated. Steam itself has more than 30K games. It is very hard to stand out or even be noticed. In this case, marketing is essential. If you are an indie developer, you might have a budget problem. I read many postmortems complaining that their marketing strategies were not efficient. Like trying to reach streamers, for example. Also, you cannot use the “if you build it, they will come” strategy. It rarely works. One thing is certain, indie developers benefit a lot from the long tail. The game will keep selling over the years. Slowly but surely.

[in] video game industry, day-one sales were just a small part of a game’s success.

This is very true, specially for indie games. It takes time to get traction. Also, it is common to add new content and bug fixes over time. Personally, I prefer to wait some months until the game is more polished so I can have a better experience playing it.

“Gungeon was going to be six months, small, just get our name out there, get some kind of income in, some kind of cohesive experience of making a game start to finish, prove we can do it, then figure out what’s next,” said Dave Rubel. “I worked on that game for five years.”

I loved this quote. Specially because the game was successful. But seriously, how to even try to foresee a scenario like this?
